# Scottsboro trials  
  
## Summary  
The Scottsboro trials were a series of legal cases in the 1930s involving nine African American teenagers falsely accused of raping two white women on a train in Alabama. The trials became a landmark in the history of the American civil rights movement and highlighted the deep-seated racism in the Southern legal system.  
## Notes  
* All nine defendants were initially convicted by all-white juries and eight were sentenced to death, despite medical evidence to the contrary.
* The Communist Party USA and the NAACP both became involved in the legal defense, which went through multiple appeals.
* The Supreme Court overturned the convictions twice in cases that established the right to adequate legal counsel and the right to a jury that does not exclude African Americans.
* The "Scottsboro Boys" spent years in prison before most were eventually released, but they were not fully pardoned until long after their deaths.

# Social Security Act  
  
## Summary  
The Social Security Act of 1935 is a landmark piece of legislation that established a federal system of old-age benefits and social insurance. It was designed to provide a safety net for the elderly, the unemployed, and disadvantaged groups.  
## Notes  
* It created a permanent national old-age pension system funded by employer and employee contributions.
* The act also provided for unemployment insurance and aid to families with dependent children.
* It was a centerpiece of the Second New Deal and fundamentally changed the relationship between the government and its citizens.
* Although it initially excluded many agricultural and domestic workers, it expanded over time to cover most of the workforce.

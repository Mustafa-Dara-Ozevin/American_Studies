# Setting  
  
## Summary  

 Time and Place. 
## Notes  
- Includes location, historical period, weather, and time of the day
- Setting creates the environments in which characters live and act
- It can influence the plot, characters, atmosphere an d meaning of the story
- A strong setting feels like an essential part of the story rather than just a background

### Atmosphere: Place, Time and Mood

1. Decide what mood you are trying to establish
2. Decide the period or moment best fits the context of your story
3. Know the atmosphere you need to potray
4. Incorporate all elements of the story
5. Use all 5 senses
6. Don't ever describe
7. Remember that the setting of a story has a direct effect on the character & plot

*The writer should choose details that support the intended mood*


### Setting and Character

- Character are influenced by their surrounded by their surroundings, culture, and historical circumstances
- The same place can affect different characters in different ways
- Setting can create conflict between a character and their environment
- writers can use the relationship between character and place to deepen characterization

### Harmony and Conflict Between Character and Place

- **Harmony:** The setting reflects a character's needs, personality pr emotional state
- **Conflict:** The setting creates discomfort, pressure, danger, or obstacles
- A character may feel at home on one environment but trapped in another
- The environment can support a character's goals or prevent them from achieving them.
- Changes in the setting can reflect the changes in the character

### Symbolic Place

- A setting can represent an idea or emotion beyond its physical characteristics 
- A locked room may symbolize, confinement or a lack of freedom

### Realistic and Imaginary setting

- realistic setting can be developed through personal experience, observation, memory, or research
- Historical setting require attention to the period's social and cultural details 
- Imaginary settings must still have recognizable characteristics and consistent rules
- Fantasy worlds still need convincing details, atmosphere and internal logic

### Narrative Time

- Narrative time concerns how the writer presents the passage of time
- Story events can be narrated at different speeds
- Some moments receive detailed attention, while longer periods may be summarized 
- The writer controls how much time readers spend experiencing the event 

| Summary                                               | Scene                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Condenses events that take place over a longer period | Presents an event in greater detail, often through action, dialog and sensory detail |

### White Space

White space refers to breaks between paragraphs or sections of a narrative
- It can indicate a shift in time and place
- Gives reader time for thinking about the story 

### Flashback

- Interrupts the present narrative to present an earlier event.
- It provides background information about characters and story

### Chapter 31

- Martin meets Russ Brissenden, who is among the guests in Mr. Morse's house.  
- Russ's careless attitude and different appearance is noticed by [[Martin Eden|Eden]].  
- "The High priestess of Mystery", "Cradle of Beauty" writing about the philosophy of beauty  
- Russ looked anemic and feather-brained. He isolates himself and does not pay attention to courtesy rules. However he can socialize successfully when he wants.  
- Martin does not like him at first because he seems offensive and cynical.  
- Martin is invited to a bar by Russ. He accepts the invitation despite not liking him because he is lonely  
- While they talk [[Martin Eden|Eden]] is astonished by Russ's intellectual prowess. He was the second intellectual he saw since Prof. Caldwell, but he had what Caldwell lacked: the passion and words of a poet.  
- Eden thinks he finally found his soulmate in Russ, which he can share the beauty of life.
- **Wandered above the sea of fog painting** ⇒ A composition foggy high cliffs and among one of the zeniths man whose back is turned to viewer appears to walking with a cane. Exclusive and individual level of understanding of life and existence. Has no duty to inform the society.   
- [[Martin Eden|Eden]] sees Russ as another person who can rise about the fog and reach zenith. 
- "You are strong like a lion, Greek God" - Russ to Eden. It can be interpreted as a bromance.
- Russ Brissenden is placeholder for [[George Sterling]] who is one of [[Jack London]]'s close friend. Prominent poet of [[Bohemianism]]. American poet who is in love with [[San Fransico]] which he famously described as "The cold ". Celebrated as Greatest poem written by a American Poet.
- George Sterling struggled with alcoholism and was in poor health.  
- Russ Brissenden, a *lunger*[^1], carried cyanide and stated, "Prison becomes a home when you have the key."  
- There was repressed homoerotic(non-sexual) attraction between them.  
- Russ was sufficiently astute to recognize Martin's reality. He understood that his ambition and devotion to love would lead to an unfortunate conclusion.
- He believes he needs to find something to connect Martin to life, so he won't leave him alone when he does.

> "Love beauty for it's own sake", "And leave the magazines alone, back to your ships and your sea -- that is my advice to you".  

- [ ] Study Love-cycle-Brandon-Eden dynamic 
- [[Martin Eden|Eden]] has a advantage of having such a physically active and adventurous life in his youth. While Bourgeois and Russ lacked the experience
- [[Herman Melville]] is a similar example but his character have more of a wise tone compared to [[Martin Eden]].

### Chapter 36

- Brissender introduce Martin to underground intellectuals who live in a worker ghetto, free from societal expectations.
- They welcome Martin and he is glad to met them as well. 
- Martin enjoys their talks but does not feel a connection. Reasons may be:
	- Martin is individualistic not comfortable in commune life
	- He still has the drive to achieve things in his life while Underground thinkers have seen their share and settled
	- He has to unclimb the social letter he climbed to get to their level which is conflicting because he worked so hard to climb
	- It conflicts with his sense of beauty just like opera singers did
### Chapter 37

- [[Nietzche]] is the new obsession of [[Martin Eden|Eden]]. He has a heated argument with judge Blount which he accuses everyone but himself of being communists. 
- [[Martin Eden]] is only exact representation of Übermensch in literature.
- [[Nietzsche]] never created a character which directly represented Übermemnsch only ones that prophetize it like ([[Zharathustra]] and Mad-man). 

- ***On Übermensch*** ⇒
	- Man is a rope stretched between. The animal and the superman - a rope over the abyss
	- A dangerous crossing, a dangerous wayfaring, a dangerous looking-back, a dangerous trembling and Halting
	- What is great in man is that he **is a bridge** and **not a goal**: what is lovable in a man is that he is an **OVER-GOING** and a **DOWN-GOING**. 

- The Last man ≠ The Übermensch
- Last man is the opposite of the [[Übermensch]] it is people who gave up their ambition and hope.

# Revenue Act  
  
## Summary  
The Revenue Act of 1935, often called the "Wealth Tax Act," was a New Deal legislative measure that significantly increased taxes on high-income earners and large corporations. It was part of FDR's strategy to address economic inequality and fund relief programs.  
## Notes  
* The act introduced a graduated income tax with rates as high as 75% for the wealthiest individuals.
* It also increased taxes on corporate profits and inheritances.
* FDR pushed for the act to counter the popularity of more radical "Share Our Wealth" movements.
* Critics labeled it "soak the rich" legislation, while supporters saw it as a necessary step for social justice.

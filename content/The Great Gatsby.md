# The Great Gatsby  
  
## Summary  
F. Scott Fitzgerald's 1925 novel exploring themes of wealth, class, and the corruption of the American Dream in Jazz Age Long Island.

## Notes  
  
### The American Dream
- Land of opportunity if you work hard and have the will to succeed.
- Rags to riches story.
- Jay Gatsby: Self-creation, the "American Adam." A self-made man who rises to immense wealth. 
- Misunderstanding reality by trying to turn back time.
- Critiques the American Dream: Is it truly open to everyone?

### Social Class and Division
- **West Egg vs. East Egg**: New money vs. old money.
- The American Dream is not truly open to everyone; social class remains a barrier.

### Hollowness of the Upper Class and Moral Decay
- Characters are corrupted by worldly wealth and power.
- Wealth often leads to moral decay. 
- Tom Buchanan: Unfaithful, driven by money and egocentrism.
- Daisy Buchanan: Symbolizes the gluttony of the upper class; more concerned about her comfort than morality. 
- Material Love: Daisy left Gatsby and married Tom for his money, showing her materialistic nature.
- Success without values leads to emptiness and destruction.

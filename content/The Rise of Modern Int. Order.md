### Historical Int orders

The historical origins of international orders may be traced to the period when nomadic groups first settled.

- These communities generated regularized practices of exchange between political units.
- Many of these historical international orders developed through encounters with other parts of the world.

The Eurasian int order between the 13th and 17th c., spanning from Europe to China, which sustained deep interactions between diverse political orders and, in the process, helped to forge notions of sovereignty and world order.

Most accounts of int order, however, begin not in 13th c. Eurasia or early modern South Asia, but in 17th c. Europe. 
The majority of accounts date the birth of ‘modern’ international order to a specific date—the 1648 Peace of Westphalia, which marked the end of the wars of religion in Europe.
- Westphalia is seen as important because it instituted the principle of cuius regio, eius religio (‘whose realm, their religion’).
- Westphalia is seen as important because it established the principle of ‘sovereign territoriality’ (a claim to political authority over a particular geographical space).

A number of criticisms of the Westphalian narrative have emerged in recent years. 
- First, Westphalia was not a European-wide agreement, but a local affair.
- Second, A dual constitutional structure that stressed loyalty to the Empire which adjudicated over both inter-state disputes and internal affairs (a bit like the modern-day European Union) continued to exist. 

We must first identify what is modern int. order before identifying the start of modern int. order.

Although we can speak of many regional int orders before the 19th c., we should locate the emergence of a distinctly modern int order only in the last two centuries.

- During this period, multiple regional int orders were linked in a global order in which all parts of the world were closely connected. 
- This period is sometimes known as the ‘[[global transformation]]’: a term used to denote the shift from a world of multiple regional int systems to one characterized by a global int order. 
- The global transformation ended a long period in which human history was mainly local and contact among peoples fairly light. 
- The 19th c. saw the transformation of the daily condition of peoples nearly everywhere on the planet.

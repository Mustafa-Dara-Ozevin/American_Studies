# first hundred days  
  
## Summary  
The first hundred days of Franklin D. Roosevelt's presidency in 1933 were characterized by an unprecedented burst of legislative activity. This period laid the groundwork for the New Deal, aiming to provide immediate relief, recovery, and reform during the Great Depression.  
## Notes  
* FDR called a special session of Congress immediately after taking office to address the banking crisis.
* Major legislation passed included the Emergency Banking Act, the Agricultural Adjustment Act (AAA), and the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC).
* The goal was to provide "Relief, Recovery, and Reform" to a desperate nation.
* The success of this period established a benchmark by which future presidents' early performances are measured.

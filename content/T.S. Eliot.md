# T.S. Eliot  
  
## Summary  
American-born British poet, essayist, and playwright; a central figure in Modernist poetry, best known for "The Waste Land" and "The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock."

## Notes  
  
### General Information
- Born in 1888.
- One of the most influential figures in poetry in his time.
- Wrote about complex emotional disconnection in humans.

### Historical Context
- Grew up during WW1.
- His youth was marked by the violence and war of the era.
- Explored themes of man being stripped of their identity.

### Impersonality and the Platinum Catalyst
- Poetry should express universal ideas rather than personal emotions.
- Poet uses their emotions as a source, but they should not dominate the art.
- In his essay "Tradition and Individual Talent" (one of the most important texts of 20th-century literary criticism).

### Tradition and the Historical Sense
- **New definition of Tradition**: Tradition is not something dead in the past; it is a living and dynamic structure. The poet must understand the entire literary history from Homer to his own time.
- **Historical Sense**: The poet must feel the past and present at the same time.
- **Simultaneous Order**: New works change the meaning of older works; no work has meaning alone.

### Major Works
- **The Waste Land**: His most famous work. Depicts a desolate world after the war, left soulless and empty. Features abstract imagery, repetition, and complex references.
- **The Hollow Men**: Disturbing but straightforward imagery. People are in a frozen state between alive and dead; the slow diminishment of the soul.
- **The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock**
- **Four Quartets**
- **Ash-Wednesday**

### Effect on Modernism
- One of the pioneers of Modernism.
- Utilized abstract imagery and collage-like narration.

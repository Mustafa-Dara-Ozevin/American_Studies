## Bertolt Bretch 

Believed there needs to be a new theatre to handle new political realilites 
Believed one must imcomprate multiple theories to his play.
	"A man with one theory is lost, He need several of them, four, lots."
The term most used by bretch for his theories was "Epic theatre"
- **Historisierung** examining a given social system from the standpoint of a scoial system from another period 
- Rejected the Aristotle's distinction between epic and tragic poetry, stressing the importance of narrative in his conception of drama as episodic narrative

Reveal the conditions which people lives in but not in a naturalist sense. It was on the contrary to illustrate Marxist dialectic. **"Social being determines thought but human is alterable and able to alter"**

It aimed to illicit a reaction from the viewer and encourage debate.

He alianeted the viewer from the play by use of music, slide projection etc

## Antonin Artaud
![[Pasted image 20260218135736.png]]
- does not fit into any school easily. his theory consist of manifestos for the two short-lived theatrical ventures and in numeruos essays and lectures. 
- Protest against the idea of culture as set apart as a binary conflict
- The theatre must act like a plaque, an all-engulfing experience from which few emerge alive but those wo do are changed for ever. 
- Written language has no place in the profound theatre insted a new language based on signs not words needs to be used
- Theatre as a religious ceremony
- **Theatre of cruelty** a theatre of rigour that would shock people out of complacent materialsm and remind people that "Sky can still fall on our heads"


# Existiantalism 

## Arthur Adamov

![[Pasted image 20260218140635.png]]
post world war 2 era absurdist theatre. Aimed to make viewer question their existence. 
- "Theatre as I understand it, is linked and utterly linked to performance"
- Metaphysical realities must manifest in physical, literal form 
- reduced language value
- vague time and place 
- uses:
	- Farcical situations
	- pauses
	- miming
	- silences
- which reflect a sense of anguish

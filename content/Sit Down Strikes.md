# Sit Down Strikes  
  
## Summary  
A sit-down strike is a form of civil disobedience where workers take possession of their workplace by sitting down at their stations, effectively preventing strikebreakers from entering and continuing production. This tactic was famously and successfully used during the 1936-1937 General Motors strike in Flint, Michigan.  
## Notes  
* Unlike traditional strikes, workers remained inside the factory, which made it difficult for police or hired guards to remove them without damaging the machinery.
* The Flint Sit-Down Strike led to the recognition of the United Auto Workers (UAW) by General Motors, a major victory for organized labor.
* The success of this tactic led to a massive wave of sit-down strikes across various industries in the late 1930s.
* The Supreme Court eventually ruled sit-down strikes illegal in 1939, as they violated property rights.

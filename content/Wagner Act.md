# Wagner Act  
  
## Summary  
The Wagner Act, officially the National Labor Relations Act of 1935, is a foundational statute of United States labor law that guarantees the right of private sector employees to organize into trade unions. It also established the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB).  
## Notes  
* It prohibited employers from engaging in "unfair labor practices," such as interfering with union formation.
* The act empowered the NLRB to conduct secret-ballot elections to determine if employees wanted union representation.
* It significantly increased the power and membership of labor unions across the country.
* The Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of the act in 1937, a major victory for the New Deal.

## Act One:

- Ibsen believed woman were best suited for being wives and mothers, at the same time he had a eye for injustice. Helmer's demeaning behavior of Nora shows that

The play opens at Christmas time. The introduction reveals many fact about the family. They live in a luxurious 

- The first subject to be discussed in the play is how Nora spends a lot of money and is irresponsible about it. 
- Helmer calls Nora with pet names such as **little lark** or **my squirrel**. The perception or role given to woman in society is hinted with such pet names.
- Helmer's study room (Masculine space)
- Piano, Deluxe edition books => high class
- Nora eating macaroons in secret + hiding this like a child + pet names => nationalization of Nora by her husband and Society.
- The first **foreshadowing**: Nora states that they always borrow money, Helmer gets upset by this idea and again criticizes her.
	- Helmer pulls her ear as if she were a child
	- Second how he correlates her proposition to borrow money with her being a women and thus irresponsible
	- Relationship Helmer describes between debt, freedom, home and beauty
- Mrs Linde entrance(Second woman character in the play) creates a comparison point for the situation of the money women in the society. 
- While some women are like Nora, infantalized and stay at home wives, others like Mrs. Linde who actually has o work to make a living. 
- In their conservation (page 34-38), we see that Nora brags about Helmer's new position in the bank and how they will live more prosperously. 
- Mrs Linde marries out of necessity due to her mother and younger brother's situation. 
- She feels aimless after her husband and mother dies and her brothers grows up. She feels aimless
- Nora feels Mrs Linde just like the others does not take her serious
- Nora secretly took a loan behind Helmer's back. She claimed her father gave the money however she actually forged his signature to barrow money from Mr. Krogstad.
- Working, making money => almost like being a man
- Dr Rank has sexually transmitted which he inherited at birth. He will have a short life span because of this. Hereditary aspect of Naturalism.
- Good intentions vs rule of law: one of the main conflicts of the play
- Nora thinks Helmer will try to sacrifice himself for her and be grateful fto her but Helmer's reaction is complete opposite. 
- Helmer proclaiming "I am saved" upon receiving the second letter causes Nora to realize her love isn't reciprocated 
- "I wouldn't be a man if I didn't love you twice as much for your womanly helplessness" - Helmer
- Nora realizes she has never been herself, she has always been a play doll that plays the role given to her. First in Daddy's house then Helmer's house
- Nora decided that she needs to leave Helmer to self-actualize and find her true self

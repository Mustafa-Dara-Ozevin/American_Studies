# Point of View  
  
## Summary  
Who speaks? To whom? In what form? at what distance? 
## Notes

 - POV is not simply a character's opinion or attitude
 * Think of it as a vantage point: Who is positioned to observe and narrate the scene?
 * It shapes the relationship among writer, characters and reader.
 * For Questions
	 * Who speaks
	 * To whom
	 * In what form
	 * At what distance 

### Omniscience: Joe and the baby

1. Joe glares at the screaming baby
2. Another Mind: The baby is frightened by his scowl
3. Interpretation: Joe's feeling is not really hatred
4. Movement in Time: Joe himself screamed like that two years earlier
5. Generalization: "Children can't tell hatred from fear"

### Limited Omniscience: One consciousness
- The author reports external events and has access to one character's mind
- It is especially useful in short stories
- it controls the focus and helps prevents awkward POV shifts 

### Objective narration: Show the evidence

- Restricts narration to externally observable facts.
- The reader recives what can be seen, hear, smelled, tasted or touched
- there is no direct revelation of the characters thoughts and no explanatory comments. 
- Example: [[Ernest Hemingway]] "Hills like White Elephants"
- Effect: readers discover what is happening rather than being told what to think

### Second person: making "you" the character

- The readers is assigned a character's perceptions and reactions. 
- Can draw the reader into an intimate an intimate, participatory position 
- Difficult to sustain
